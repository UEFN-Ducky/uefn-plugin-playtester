The game

Fortnite: Play Tester plays the real Fortnite window that UEFN opens. In UEFN press Launch Session and wait for the island to load.

Other games: start the game; Play Tester finds its window again by its program or title. A Godot game runs from the editor with the runtime probe, a web game in a Browser tab. This turns green by itself.

Testers

Each tab is one tester playing one game, with its own runs and its own Who answers. + opens another: Fortnite, Godot, Unreal, Roblox, a web page or any open window.

Testers run at the same time. Games in desktop windows share your keyboard and mouse, so they take turns pressing keys (a tab says waiting meanwhile). Godot and web testers never wait.

Jev

Jev is the vision model that looks at every frame and answers Play Tester's questions, like "is the target on screen?". It uses your TypeSafe key in Settings → LLMs, or Jev through Ducky AI when you are signed in; Settings → Play Tester → Who answers can pick a gateway model instead.

"With box" means it can also draw a box around the target.

Beacon

A small Verse device in your island. A few times a second it writes where the player is and which way they face to the UEFN log, and Play Tester reads it.

With it, the bot knows how far and which way the target is, even behind a wall, behind the player or on another floor. Without it, Jev steers by the picture only.

Agents add it by themselves before a play test when it's off. You can also ask Ducky in chat: Add the Play Tester beacon to my island. It stays in the level after that.

The agent's orders

Agents start play tests from chat with playtester_run. This card shows what the agent asked for: the goal, the target, when it passes and the limits.

Show exact orders opens the stored call, the questions Jev answers, the rules the bot used, the log and the settings. Stop ends a run at once.

What Jev saw

The frame Jev looked at in each step. The orange box is where Jev found the target, the arrow is the turn, and the chips at the bottom are the keys pressed.

Click a step in the strip (or use ← →) to look back. Live follows the newest step.

Jev's move

What the bot did this step and why, in its own words. The purple tag is the rule that picked the move.

The bars are Jev's answers from 0 to 100%. Answers under the confidence setting count as no. Step data shows the exact keys, mouse counts and Jev's raw answers.

Position

Where the player and the target are in the world, from the beacon. The map is seen from above with the player in the middle facing up. The orange dot is the target and grey dots are landmarks.

x, y and z are in centimetres. No beacon means no positions: the Beacon help says how to add it. Other games steer by the picture; Godot testers report node positions to agents.

Pressing

The keys and mouse the bot used this step. Lit keys were pressed; outlined ones were only planned (dry run).

When no run is playing, click a key to press it for 0.3 s, or the left or right half of the dial to turn 30°.
